Mac software upgrade

£80

Apple stops releasing new macOS versions for older Macs, even when they still work well. Browsers and apps then stop supporting the old one.

I use OpenCore Legacy Patcher, an independent open-source project, to install a newer macOS. How well it works depends on your model, so I check yours first.

What's involved

  • Check your Mac suits the upgrade
  • Install a newer macOS with OpenCore Legacy Patcher
  • A basic test afterwards, to check the essentials work
  • Hand your Mac back with a short summary of what works and what doesn't
Good to know
  • Apple doesn't endorse this method.
  • Features that need an Apple silicon or T2 security chip won't work. iPhone Mirroring is one example.
  • Some macOS updates may need extra steps afterwards.
How long it takes
  • There isn't one number, and it varies from Mac to Mac.
  • Some of the time I'm working on the Mac, some of the time I'm waiting beside it while something installs, and some of the time it runs by itself.
  • Ask me about your Mac and I'll give you an idea.
What I need from you
  • I need the password of an administrator account on your Mac.
  • If you'd rather not give me yours, create a new administrator account (in Users & Groups, in System Settings or System Preferences) and give me that password. You can delete the account afterwards.
  • Please don't email passwords. I'll tell you how to pass it on when you book.

Back-up

+ £20

Before I change anything, I make a full copy of your whole Mac with Time Machine, Apple's own back-up tool. It goes on my encrypted SSD.

If something goes wrong, I can wipe the Mac, start again and put everything back. That happened once, on the MacBook Air in my portfolio, and the back-up put it right.

What's involved

  • Copy your whole Mac with Time Machine
  • Check the back-up is complete before I start
  • Keep it on my encrypted SSD for 14 days after I hand your Mac back, then delete it (or sooner, if you ask)
Good to know
  • It's an add-on to the Mac software upgrade, and it's included in Go Full Neobitium.
  • If Time Machine won't work (rare), I tell you and copy your files across by hand in Finder instead.
  • That protects your files, but isn't a full copy of the whole Mac.

When it's not worth it.

I'd rather tell you not to book than take your money for an upgrade that won't help. These are the common reasons. If any of them sound like your Mac, email me first.

Your Mac already does what you need

  • Apple usually still sends security updates for the newest macOS and the two before it. If your Mac is on one of those, it's still being looked after.
  • Your browser and the apps you use still work and still update.
  • You only need a newer browser. Some browsers keep supporting older macOS versions for longer than Safari does.

The Mac itself is the problem

  • It has a spinning hard drive and 4 GB memory or less. A newer macOS will feel slow, and an SSD would help more than any software. I don't fit parts.
  • It has a hardware fault: random shutdowns, glitchy graphics, no Wi-Fi, a drive that grinds or clicks. A newer macOS won't fix those, and can make them easier to notice. Get it repaired first.
  • The battery is swollen or bulging. Stop using it and get it looked at. That's a safety problem, not a software one.
  • It has liquid damage or a broken screen. I don't do repairs.
  • It's very old, or its graphics are very old. Some Macs aren't supported by OpenCore Legacy Patcher at all. Others can run a newer macOS, but without proper graphics support windows and animations can be slow and glitchy. Tell me the model and I'll say what to expect.
  • The drive is nearly full. A newer macOS needs room to install and run, so you'd need to clear some space first.

It's not the right job if…

  • You need a guarantee. Apple doesn't endorse this method, and a macOS update can occasionally need patching again. It isn't right for a Mac your work or business can't be without.
  • It's a work or school Mac, or its rules or device management don't allow changes. If it's locked to someone else's iCloud account or has a firmware lock, I can't help.
  • You need an old app that only runs on an old macOS. For example, 32-bit apps stop working from macOS Catalina onwards. If you use Windows through Boot Camp, tell me first, because it may not survive the upgrade.
  • You want features that need an Apple silicon or T2 chip, like iPhone Mirroring or Apple Intelligence. No software can add those to an older Mac.
  • You want the best speed and the newest features. A newer second-hand Mac can be better value than upgrading an old one.
What I do about it
  • I check your Mac before I start, and if I think it isn't worth it, I say so before you pay anything.
  • If I find out during the job that your Mac can't run what we agreed, I stop and refund you in full, not including postage costs.
